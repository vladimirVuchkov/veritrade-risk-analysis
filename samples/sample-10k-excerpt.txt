NORTHWIND INDUSTRIAL CORP.
ANNUAL REPORT ON FORM 10-K FOR THE FISCAL YEAR ENDED DECEMBER 31, 2025

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Risks Related to Our Financial Condition

Our independent registered public accounting firm has included an explanatory paragraph in its report stating that our recurring losses raise substantial doubt about our ability to continue as a going concern. In connection with the audit of our consolidated financial statements, management identified a material weakness in our internal control over financial reporting related to revenue recognition. We have a substantial amount of indebtedness, and a breach of our financial covenants could result in an event of default under our credit agreement. We recorded goodwill impairment charges of $42.1 million (approximately 18% of total goodwill) during the fourth quarter.

Risks Related to Legal Matters

We are a defendant in a securities class action lawsuit filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York. We are also subject to pending litigation with a former distributor, and an adverse judgment could require us to pay substantial damages. From time to time we receive claims of infringement from holders of patents.

Risks Related to Our Operations

We rely on a limited number of suppliers for critical components, and a supply chain disruption could delay shipments. The loss of key personnel could harm our business. Natural disasters or a pandemic could interrupt our manufacturing facilities.

Risks Related to Cybersecurity

In March 2025 we experienced a ransomware attack that encrypted parts of our corporate network. We cannot rule out unauthorized access to customer data in future incidents, and phishing attempts against our employees are increasing.

Risks Related to Regulation

We are cooperating with an SEC investigation concerning our historical revenue practices. Our international operations are subject to the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and export control regulations.

Risks Related to Our Markets

We face intense competition from larger companies. An economic downturn or foreign currency fluctuations could reduce demand for our products, and inflationary pressures continue to raise our costs. The market price of our common stock may be volatile.
